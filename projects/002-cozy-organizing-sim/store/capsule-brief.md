## 1. The game in one paragraph

You are a professional organizer. Clients' handbags, backpacks, drawers and toolboxes are tipped onto your desk. You sort the chaos into a perfect **knolling** flat-lay: objects at right angles, evenly spaced and grouped by type or colour. Then you photograph it and pack everything back in so the lid closes. The view is **strictly top-down, flat 2D**. There are no visible characters; clients appear only as Polaroids and handwritten notes. The mood is cozy, tactile and deeply satisfying: "chaos to order".

Greybox screenshots are in `../assets/greybox/`. `02-tip.png` shows the mess, `06-score-reveal.png` the finished grid with its score. Final item sprites will be flat top-down objects with soft drop shadows. The style test of the first 10 sprites is scheduled for Nov 30 – Dec 4 and will be sent as soon as it exists. **The capsule must match the in-game look** (§5), so the artist and the sprite style test should talk in week 1.

The title is **not locked yet**. See `name-check.md`. The final name is due by **Fri Dec 4**. Start on composition and illustration; start the logo lettering only after Dec 4.

## 3. What must read at small sizes

Test every capsule at **184×69** (auto-generated) and **231×87** (half of #2), on both a light and a dark background, before delivery.

- **Genre at a glance:** a mess of everyday objects becoming a perfect grid. A viewer who never reads the title should still get "organizing / tidy-up / satisfying".
- **Logo legible at 184×69:**
  - Thick, simple letterforms; no thin serifs or script.
  - Two words at most on the main line. "Co." can be smaller, but not tiny.
  - High contrast against the background behind it.
- **One focal subject:** the grid, or the bag spilling into the grid. Not ten competing objects.
- **Readable silhouettes:** 6–12 large, iconic objects (scissors, keys, a pencil, a phone, an apple, a tape measure). Don't use 60 tiny ones at capsule scale; use texture in the larger formats only.
- **Colour separation:** the desk mat is a saturated mid-tone, the objects are a bright colour-blocked palette, and the logo sits on its own colour bar or label. Our greybox uses a green cutting mat, which is fine to keep or replace.

Mood words: **warm daylight, tactile, crisp, quietly funny, "ahh"**.
Palette:
- Desk: warm wood or a saturated cutting mat.
- Objects: candy-bright but not neon.
- Shadows: soft, from the upper left, matching in-game.

## 5. Do / don't

**Do**
- Make it look like the game. The capsule's objects, angle (straight overhead) and shadow style must match the screenshots, or the store page feels like a bait-and-switch.
- Show contrast: mess and order in the same image.
- Use straight lines and even gaps deliberately; right angles are our brand.
- Leave safe margins for Steam overlays: discount badges sit bottom-left on some capsules, so check the [asset page](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/assets/standard).
- Build every size from one layered master so the set feels identical.
- Design a logo that can also work as a **label-maker strip or masking-tape label**. It will be reused in the trailer end card and social avatars.

**Don't**
- No AI-generated imagery at any stage, including the underpainting, references-as-base or upscaling. The capsule, logo and key art are declared human-made in our Steam AI disclosure (`store-page.md` §8). Purely prompted output is likely not copyrightable in the US [M:tech/steam-ai-disclosure-player-facing-only]. **This is a contract term.**
- No real brands or logos on objects. No Knoll furniture or anything resembling the Knoll, Inc. trademark style (`name-check.md`).
- No people's faces or hands as the focal point. There are no characters in the game; a Polaroid of a client in a corner is OK.
- No text other than the logo: no taglines, review quotes or "cozy organizing game" on the capsule. Valve's rules restrict text on capsules other than the title; check the current rule on the asset page.
- No 3D perspective, isometric view or rooms.
- Don't make it so busy it reads as a hidden-object game.

## 6. Three compositions

The artist sketches all three as thumbnails. We pick one by Fri Dec 4.

**Sketch 1: "Spill to grid" (recommended to try first)**
- The camera is straight overhead on the desk. An open backpack sits at the left edge, tipped over, with objects spilling out in a messy diagonal stream.
- Moving right, the stream straightens into a perfect knolled grid: same objects, right angles, even gaps, grouped by colour.
- The logo sits in the clean right half as a label-maker strip laid square on the mat.
- It reads at thumbnail size as chaos on the left and order on the right, which is the trailer's first 3 seconds frozen in one image.
- Wide formats crop easily. In the vertical formats (#4, #5), run the spill from top to bottom: mess above, grid below, logo at the bottom.

**Sketch 2: "The knolled bag"**
- Symmetrical: the emptied bag is centred and flattened like an exhibit. Its contents are knolled around it in a perfect frame, like a museum display, with one item still mid-air or tilted (just placed, about to snap).
- The logo goes centre-top on a paper tag tied to the bag handle.
- Very strong at small size because of the symmetry, and it shows "satisfying" well.
- Risk: it shows only order, not the mess, so it loses the transformation.

**Sketch 3: "The photo"**
- A finished flat-lay on the desk, captured at the instant of the camera flash: a white bloom at one corner and a Polaroid sliding out into the foreground.
- The Polaroid shows the same layout with a red rubber-stamp "NEAT" mark (an in-game beat).
- The logo is written on the Polaroid's white strip, as if in marker.
- Story-forward and warm: it says "job" and "client".
- Risk: a photo-in-a-photo can read as muddy at 184×69. The logo would need to escape the Polaroid onto the mat for the small capsule.

Pick criteria:
1. Readable at 184×69.
2. Says "organizing" without the logo.
3. Matches the in-game sprites.
4. Works vertically.
