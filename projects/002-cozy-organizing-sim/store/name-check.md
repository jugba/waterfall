# Name check: is "Knolling Co." usable?

- Checked: 2026-11-27, from this container.
- Method:
  - Steam store search (`store.steampowered.com/search/results/?term=…`, JSON results).
  - Steam `api/appdetails` for close matches.
  - Web search, 4 queries.
- **This is not a legal clearance.** A real trademark search must be done by the user (§5) before the logo is commissioned. **Name lock is due Fri Dec 4** (`capsule-brief.md` §7).

## 1. Verdict

**Usable on Steam, subject to the user's trademark search.**

- **Steam:**
  - "knolling co" returns **0 results**.
  - "knolling", "knoll" and "knolled" return only fuzzy matches. None of their titles contain "knoll" (Puzzling Places DLC, BECROWNED, Greyfall, etc.).
  - No Steam game uses the word.
- **Web:** there is no game, studio or company called "Knolling" or "Knolling Co." The word is a generic term for the arranging practice, popularized by Tom Sachs. Sources: web search results 2026-11-27, e.g. [Brick Fanatics on LEGO Ideas' knolling activity](https://www.brickfanatics.com/lego-ideas-activity-asks-fans-to-try-knolling).
- **Trademark-looking conflict to flag: KNOLL.**
  - "Knolling" is named after Knoll furniture. Knoll, Inc., now part of MillerKnoll, holds many KNOLL registrations ([Justia: Knoll, Inc. trademarks](https://trademarks.justia.com/owners/knoll-inc-37057); [Justia: KNOLL 74454052](https://trademarks.justia.com/744/54/knoll-74454052.html)).
  - A web search on 2026-11-27 found **no "KNOLLING" mark**, only KNOLL, KNOLLER and similar (e.g. [Justia: KNOLLER](https://trademarks.justia.com/885/20/knoller-88520938.html)).
  - Our reading, not legal advice: the risk is low to medium. The marks appear to cover furniture and textiles, not games (classes 9, 28 and 41), and "knolling" is in common descriptive use.
  - Mitigation:
    - No Knoll products, logo styling or the Knoll name in the game or marketing.
    - Don't write "Knoll" alone.
- **Marketing pros:**
  - Unique on Steam, so searches for it land only on us.
  - It owns the #knolling hashtag niche.
  - The "X Co." job-sim pattern is proven: Cat Mail Co. (94%, 9.2k reviews), Lost and Found Co. (98%, 3.5k reviews) (concepts.md §1.1).
- **Marketing cons:**
  - Most players don't know the word. The short description must explain it (`store-page.md` §1 option A doesn't depend on it).
  - Pronunciation is unclear: the K is silent ("nolling").
  - It doesn't translate. The ZH-Hans store needs a descriptive localized title.

| 2 | **Knolled** | none | One short word: a cleaner, bigger logo at 184×69, same meaning and hashtag | Same KNOLL proximity as Knolling Co. |
| 3 | **Kit & Caboodle** | none exact; Critter Caboodle (coming soon) | "The whole kit and caboodle" means everything in the bag; playful and memorable | Common idiom; caboodle-named brands likely exist (e.g. cosmetic cases, unverified): check class 28/41. Weak in translation |
| 4 | **Everything Laid Out** | none | Plain-English promise of the fantasy, searchable, and it localizes well | Close to "Everything In Its Place" (Nov 2026) in rhythm; generic phrase |
| 5 | **Tip & Tidy** | none exact | Names the loop (tip it out, tidy it up), alliterative, reads at a glance | "Tidy" is a crowded word on Steam (143 releases), so less distinctive in search; "Tip & Tidy" may exist as a cleaning-product brand (unverified) |

Ranking if Knolling Co. fails the trademark check: **Flat Lay Co.**, then **Tip & Tidy**. **Knolled** only makes sense if the search clears the KNOLL root, and in that case keep Knolling Co.

Do not use:
- "Everything In Its Place" and "Everything MUST Fit" (taken).
- Anything with "Neat", "Sorted", "Unpack", "Odds & Ends" or "Bits & Bobs" (taken or confusable).
- A "Tidy Up the X" subtitle: it signals the clone wave (concepts.md §1.2).

## 4. Subtitle option

Steam search matches title words. A subtitle such as **"Knolling Co.: A Cozy Organizing Job"** would help searches for "organizing" and "cozy" and explain the word "knolling". Cost: a longer logo lockup and a less brand-like name. Recommendation: no subtitle in the logo. Put the keywords in the short description and tags; Steam search matches tags too.
